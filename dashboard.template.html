What to change

in priority order

  1. Put a floor under the week before raising the ceiling

    Three runs every week beats four-then-one. Your {{cv}}% week-to-week variation is doing more damage than your average mileage suggests. Target a floor of 20 km, every week, before chasing a 35 km week.

  2. Rebuild the long run from 11 km, not from the plan's 22

    Add roughly 2 km a week: 13, 15, 17, 19. That reaches the low end of the Phase 2b requirement by late October without a doubling jump, and it is the same base the marathon needs afterwards.

  3. Add one 1 km repeat session a week — it is literally the race

    6–8 × 1 km at target race pace with short recovery. Start it now: at {{fast_gap}} days without genuine speed work, the first few sessions rebuild a capacity you already had in April, not a new one.

  4. Log the strength and station work, or plan around its absence

    Right now the two are indistinguishable in the data. If you are lifting, record it so load and readiness mean something. If you are not, that is the larger finding — the station half of the race is untrained with 15 weeks left.

  5. Decide about hockey before October decides for you

    Either budget 4–5 sessions a month into the peak block and cut running intensity to match, or sit out the first half of the season. Both work. Being surprised by it in November does not.

  6. Wear the watch overnight

    Zero training cost, and it turns {{sleep_blind}}% of your recovery data from guesswork into measurement — right as the load starts to climb.
